Exact transit hits¶

简体中文 · Documentation · API

transit_hits finds every selected exact aspect to an existing natal chart within a UTC interval. It returns a TransitHits result with the original chart, the search inputs and model provenance, and each crossing or contact. Hit times are JDE(TT): Julian Dates on the TT time scale, not UTC clock readings.

For the natal-only Sun/Moon convenience return_hits and a chart at a chosen hit/location, see solar and lunar returns.

Search an interval¶

The fixed source may also be a MomentChart; natal_objects names its fixed-reference role, not its birth-input kind. TransitHits.epoch_policy keeps fixed_natal_degree_against_moving_of_date_longitude for both source types because the longitude convention is unchanged. The genuine source type/input remains in result.chart. For building and reading a moment chart, see moment charts.

This synthetic birth example calculates one stored natal Sun. The moving side needs only an object selection and an interval, with no birthplace or houses. Both endpoints must be timezone-aware built-in datetime values at or after 1972-01-01 UTC. Local offsets are normalized to UTC; naive values are rejected.

>>> from datetime import UTC, date, datetime, time, timedelta
>>> from astrology import (
...   BirthRecord, Body, ChartConfig, FixedOffset, HouseSystem, Location,
...   MajorAspect, natal_chart, prepare_natal_input, transit_hits,
... )
>>> record = BirthRecord(date(2000, 4, 10), time(12), FixedOffset(timedelta(0)),
...   Location(0, 0), source={"example": "synthetic"})
>>> chart = natal_chart(prepare_natal_input(record),
...   ChartConfig(HouseSystem.WHOLE_SIGN, (Body.SUN,), ()))
>>> result = transit_hits(chart,
...   start_utc=datetime(2024, 3, 1, tzinfo=UTC),
...   end_utc=datetime(2024, 6, 1, tzinfo=UTC),
...   moving_objects=(Body.MERCURY,), natal_objects=(Body.SUN,),
...   aspects=(MajorAspect.CONJUNCTION,))
>>> for hit in result.hits:
...   print(f"{hit.moving_object.value} {hit.aspect.value} {hit.natal_object.value} "
...         f"{hit.jde_tt:.5f} {hit.direction.value} {hit.longitude_rate_deg_per_tt_day:+.3f}")
mercury conjunction sun 2460392.23731 increasing +1.208
mercury conjunction sun 2460414.61236 decreasing -0.744
mercury conjunction sun 2460437.45999 increasing +0.812

Mercury reaches the fixed natal Sun degree three times: once while its longitude increases, once during retrograde motion, and once after it increases again. Rates are degrees per TT day, copied from the upstream event. A tangent event touches the requested longitude without crossing it; its reported rate need not be exactly zero.

For ordinary UTC clock display, convert the civil fields returned by jde_to_utc to a timezone-aware datetime. fraction is the elapsed fraction of that UTC day:

>>> from celestial_calendar import jde_to_utc
>>> civil = jde_to_utc(result.hits[0].jde_tt)
>>> displayed = datetime(civil.year, civil.month, civil.day, tzinfo=UTC) + timedelta(days=civil.fraction)
>>> displayed.isoformat(timespec="seconds")
'2024-03-22T17:40:34+00:00'

Retain jde_tt as the event time; this seconds-only display loses precision. A UTC-to-TT-to-UTC round trip need not recover the original civil fields at leap seconds or the 1972 model boundary, and Python datetime cannot represent a leap second.

Select targets and aspects¶

  • moving_objects selects bodies or named lunar nodes, in the given order. They need not be present on the natal chart. Axes cannot move in this query.

  • natal_objects selects stored bodies/nodes and may explicitly add Axis members. Selections follow the source’s stored position order, then Ascendant, Midheaven, Descendant and Imum Coeli. Axes use their own stored fields.

  • aspects selects MajorAspect identities, not AspectRule orbs. Nothing is inherited from the natal chart’s aspect rules. Each selection must be nonempty and duplicate-free; unavailable natal objects are rejected. Retained aspects are normalized to ascending angle order.

For example, natal_objects=(Axis.ASCENDANT,) searches the source chart’s stored Ascendant degree instead of its Sun. Import Axis from astrology and replace the natal_objects argument in the call above. Every NatalChart stores its axes, regardless of the selected bodies; this example’s axis query returns no hits in the displayed interval. A natal target stays fixed even when its stored position includes a longitude rate.

Conjunction and opposition each have one branch. The other major aspects search both oriented branches. hit.branch_deg is the signed angle added to the natal degree: 0° for conjunction, 180° for opposition, and ± the aspect angle for the others. hit.query_longitude_deg is that sum reduced to [0°, 360°), the moving longitude the search solves for. hit.longitude_deg preserves the raw event longitude, which can differ by a small numerical residual or lie on the other side of 0°/360°. See the rule and coordinate limits for the own-date longitude convention.

Search cost¶

The number of upstream interval scans is:

moving objects × natal targets × total oriented aspect branches

All five major aspects have eight branches in total, not five. Narrow each selection to the question you need, and start with a short interval. The call provides no progress callback and returns only after every selected scan succeeds; it does not return partial results.

Measurements on 2026-11-07 with celestial-calendar 1.1.1 and Python 3.14.9 on a 16-core Linux machine give approximate costs, not timing guarantees. One longitude scan over 365 TT days took about 2–11 seconds for Jupiter, Uranus, Venus, Saturn, Mars and Mercury; Mercury was slowest. Sun and Moon each took less than 0.1 second. A separate one-year search of Saturn and Jupiter against natal Sun, Moon and Ascendant, selecting conjunction, square and opposition, took about 74 seconds: 2 × 3 × (1 + 2 + 1) = 24 scans.

Ten moving bodies against ten natal bodies plus four axes, with all five aspects, require 10 × 14 × 8 = 1120 scans. Extrapolating each moving body’s measured scan time equally across all natal targets and branches gives roughly an hour for that one-year request; the complete request was not timed. Costs depend on the target, interval, machine and load; do not assume that they scale linearly with interval length from these samples.

Read and retain the result¶

>>> result.chart is chart
True
>>> result.start_utc.isoformat()
'2024-03-01T00:00:00+00:00'
>>> result.sources[0].model_kind, result.sources[0].model_identity
('position_source', 3)
>>> result.celestial_version
'1.1.1'

('position_source', 3) is the effective upstream model key for this Mercury search; result.sources[0].model_description gives its name and description.

start_jde_tt and end_jde_tt retain the converted search interval. Each TransitSource identifies the effective moving model, its event-domain bounds, maximum span and upstream descriptions. The result also retains the astrology version and explicit time, position-frame and epoch policies. These versions describe the current transit calculation; result.chart.provenance describes the original natal calculation and may record different versions.

Hits are sorted by TT time, moving selection order, natal source order, ascending aspect angle and then negative before positive branch. Different identities at the same time remain separate. Close crossings are not merged by a tolerance. Timestamps are reported as multiples of 2^-30 day; that numerical resolution is not an astronomical accuracy bound.

Sequences are isolated as immutable tuples. Constructing or reading a supplied result does not load native astronomy. See the constructor validation contract for the checks performed on caller-supplied results.

Limits and failures¶

The search interval is half-open, including its TT start and excluding its TT end. Its maximum duration is 3660 TT days, and every selected moving source must admit the whole interval. The limit applies after UTC-to-TT conversion: leap-second changes can make a civil interval of 3660 days exceed the TT cap. This is separate from the natal snapshot’s joint domain: selecting the Sun alone can search dates unavailable to Pluto. UTC conversion uses the upstream’s fixed leap-second table, so its final offset is retained after the last entry; it does not predict future leap seconds. A known leap step can make the TT duration differ from the civil elapsed duration.

Missing installed distribution metadata

importlib.metadata.PackageNotFoundError

TransitDomainError is a ValueError with operation, the moving identity and TT endpoints when available, upstream_message, and the original exception as its cause. A failed provider aborts the whole call; no partial list is returned. An empty successful hit tuple means the complete selected search found no event.

An endpoint uses the offset returned by its datetime.utcoffset(), including its fold setting. This function does not detect or reject nonexistent or ambiguous local clock readings. Resolve daylight-saving gaps or overlaps before supplying an endpoint; see the birth-time explanation.

This operation finds exact moving-to-fixed hits. It does not calculate a fixed-instant transit chart, orb-entry windows, moving-to-moving events or interpretation. For aspects at one chosen instant with local motion, use moment_chart and transit_aspects. Static cross-chart aspects remain available when no motion is wanted; both compare own-date stored longitudes under the source-compatibility convention.
